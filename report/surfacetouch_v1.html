SurfaceTouch V1

A projected wall becomes a touch surface: one phone tracks the hand with its main and ultra-wide cameras, and the finger's own projected shadow tells the system whether the finger is on the wall or in the air.

Why the shadow

The Touch Screen Study showed that the phone maps a finger onto a screen to within millimetres but can't measure its distance from the screen. The two back lenses are 1 cm apart. A finger 3 cm off the surface shifted by only 1.4 px between them, while the measurement wobbled by 3.7 px. A projector gives a much stronger cue for free: a finger in the air casts a shadow beside itself, and the shadow joins the finger only at contact.

Stereo alone: 0.66

Touch vs hover separation score from the 1 cm lens pair (0.5 = guessing, 1.0 = perfect). Too weak to drive touch.

One camera from the front: 100% false

Without depth, every hover frame lined up with the screen and would count as a touch.

Mapping: 1.5 mm

The wall-mapping step already works. Position is not the problem, contact is.

How the shadow encodes contact

  • Projected content isn't mistaken for shadow. The app remembers what it projected about 60 ms ago, warps it into the camera view, and expects dark buttons to be dark. Only light that is missing counts as shadow.
  • Camera exposure changes are absorbed. The brightness gain is re-measured every frame from wall pixels around the hand. Tested: a 40% exposure drop changes the features by less than 0.1.
  • Missing data is never a touch. If no shadow is visible the rule gives 0.5 (uncertain), below the touch threshold. Losing the hand ends any touch after 150 ms, so a touch can't stick like it did in the side-camera test (41 s).

Contact features

FeatureMeaningTouchingHovering
sep_nLit wall between finger and its shadowabout 0, attachedgrows with height
along_nShadow tip vs fingertip, along the fingerbehind the tip (hidden under the finger)level with or past the tip
area_nShadow visible near the tipsmalllarger
gap_n, near_n, dx_n, dy_nDistance and direction from tip to shadowshortlonger, in the projector's direction
parallax_v, parallax_yStereo shift of the fingertip between the two lenses (supporting)weak at 1 cm; the model learns how much to trust it

All lengths are divided by the finger's own tip-to-knuckle length, so the features don't change when the hand is nearer to or further from the phone. A logistic model combines them. Its threshold is set where hover false alarms stay at 2% or less on the training data, so it prefers missing a touch to inventing one.

Touch states

IDLE ──hand on wall──▶ HOVER ──p ≥ threshold──▶ CANDIDATE ──held 100 ms──▶ TOUCH_DOWN
                          ▲                         │ drops                      │ moves > 1.5% → DRAG
                          └────────────────────────-┘                            ▼
                     TOUCH_UP (+ CLICK if short and still) ◀──p < 0.6·threshold for 120 ms──┘
     hand lost ≥ 150 ms while down ──▶ CANCEL ──▶ IDLE                  (never a stuck touch)

Every event is logged in the format from the brief: {"event": "TOUCH_DOWN", "pointer_id": 1, "x": 0.42, "y": 0.67, "confidence": 0.96, "timestamp": …}, with x and y as 0–1 of the projected image. Live mode turns them into Mac mouse clicks and drags, and a sideways flick into next or previous slide.

Projector test plan · 4 Nov

  1. Setup: projector as an extended display, lights dimmed, phone on a mount low and to one side about 1–1.5 m from the wall. Not beside the projector lens, or the shadow hides behind the finger. Measure the projected width for --wall-cm.
  2. Calibrate: 15 markers, then a white flash with hands away from the wall. Takes about 3 s.
  3. Experiment A, touch vs hover (the deciding one): 6 positions × touch, 10, 20, 30, 50 mm, 2 s each. Three rounds with different finger angles, about 300 labelled frames per condition.
  4. Experiment B, accuracy: 5 × 5 grid + 6 hover traps. Reports detection, false touches, error in mm (median, p95, max), latency.
  5. Experiment C, real use: buttons, drawing, dragging, slider, then Keynote in live mode.
  6. Optional: repeat A and B with room lights on.

Design decisions

Brief suggestedV1 doesWhy
StereoSGBM disparity mapFingertip parallax as a learned featureA 1 cm baseline gives about 1.4 px per 3 cm, and the two lenses differ in field of view. A dense depth map would mostly be noise. The model's weight on the stereo feature shows whether it helps.
Checkerboard lens calibrationHomography from 15 projected markersThe wall is flat, so a plane mapping is exact enough (1.5 mm fit before) and needs no printed board.
Fixed thresholds (0.90 confidence)Threshold learned per setup at ≤ 2% false alarmsProbabilities depend on the room, wall and projector. A fixed 0.80 missed every touch in the dry run.
FastAPI + WebSocket to a browserJSON-lines events + Mac mouse and keysThe same event format, so a browser canvas can subscribe later without touching the engine.

Risks

  • Shadow hidden behind the finger when the phone sits near the projector lens. The setup screen says so. The diagnostics window shows the shadow live, so the placement can be fixed before testing.
  • Over-exposed wall leaves no room to see shadows. The app warns if the white reference is close to clipping.
  • Low-angle hand tracking: 51% at the top of a monitor in the last study. On a big wall the phone should sit further back.
  • Single finger only in V1. Multi-touch needs a pointer per hand and per-finger shadows.
