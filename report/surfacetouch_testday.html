Every session run today (11:12–12:15) with the Epson projector, the POCO M4 Pro phone and, for part of the day, the Mac camera. All numbers come from the session logs and recorded video in logs/surface_20261104_*.

Result: wall mapping works, touch detection does not yet

The cameras find the projected wall reliably, and with the new finger lock the pointer lands on the dot in 84% of held touches. Telling a touch from a hover failed in every run: the best run found 72% of touches but also fired 16 times on 6 hover targets. With the phone beside the projector, the camera sees the wall from almost where the light comes from, so the finger's shadow hides behind the finger. The phone can give position; touch needs a second viewpoint.

The index tip is usually right: its median error is 1.4–1.9% of the wall width. But in a third of held touches the thumb landed nearer the dot, which is the confusion you saw. The hand was tracked in 65–82% of frames depending on the cue.

Fix tested on recorded video: finger lock

The finger lock picks the most extended finger as the pointer and keeps it unless another finger is clearly more extended for 0.3 s. Replayed on the 12:12 video, on frames where you held still on a touch target:

In 65 frames the lock chose the point the tracker had named "thumb", and those frames were on the dot: the tracker sometimes labels your pointing finger as the thumb. The lock is now on in the live app.

Why the training data was not good enough

Experiment A

It asks for hovers at 10, 20, 30 and 50 mm. Nobody can hold a finger at a measured height in the air, so every hover label was a guess, and the model learned from guesses.

Experiment T

The new test takes its label from a mouse button or Shift held in the other hand exactly while the finger touches. You can always feel a touch, so the label is exact. Today's run recorded no presses, so it produced no labels.

The button reader works when tested on its own. Whether the button wasn't pressed or the press didn't reach the app can't be told from the logs, so the test now waits for one press and release.

No mouse

If no press comes within 8 seconds, labels come from the instructions, kept only where you visibly obey them: still and on the dot for a touch, still and close for a hover. Moving frames are dropped. On the 12:12 video this kept 312 of 2,537 frames (189 touch, 123 hover).

With Shift or a mouse

Still the more exact option. It keeps frames while you move, which gives about three times more data per session.

Changed today

  • Sensor zoom (--zoom): the phone stays by the projector, and its 4624 × 3472 sensor is cropped to the wall plus a margin, up to 3×. Checked on the phone: the crop applies to both lenses (2× gave 2312 × 1736 sensor pixels in the 960 × 720 stream). Not yet used in a session.
  • Recording: every session saves phone, ultra-wide, Mac and review video, every calibration, and a frames.csv with cue, target, button, touch probability and state for each frame.
  • Replay: re-runs tracking and touch signals on a recorded session and scores them, with a scan for the phone/Mac time offset. A 3-minute session takes about 90 s.
  • Finger lock on by default (above).
  • Mac camera as a second view. Finds the same 15 markers, maps the fingertip onto the wall, and measures how far the two views disagree.
  • Wall-crop tracking. Each camera tracks the hand only inside the wall area plus a margin, so the hand looks bigger to the tracker.
  • Experiment T (key E): 8 spots × HOLD, NEAR, TAP, WAVE, labelled by the button, with frames within 150 ms of a press or release dropped. It reports the thumb/index confusion and trains the model. Old Experiment A moved to key A.
  • Experiment B saves every frame, so missed touches can be explained afterwards.
  • Tests: 8 automated checks pass, including the two-view signal and the Teach labelling.
