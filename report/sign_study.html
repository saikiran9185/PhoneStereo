Three-Camera Sign Study

How accurately and how fast are six hand signs recognised by a single Mac camera, by the phone's two back cameras, and by all three cameras together? Tested on the same hand movements, against cues with known timing.

3 sessions90 cued signs~8,000 frames per camera1 participantphone on USBMediaPipe Hand Landmarker

Findings

The Mac camera alone was the best single setup

94% of cues recognised, 85% of settled frames correct and the fastest response. The phone's pair scored 89% and 78%.

Three cameras matched the Mac but did not beat it

With fusion learned fairly (never from the session being scored), all three cameras scored the same as the Mac. The difference was −0.1 points, 95% CI −0.3 to 0.

The thumb causes most errors

THUMBS UP is read as FIST: the Mac gets it right in 57% of frames and the phone in 9%. Every other sign is 86–98% correct on all setups.

The phone adds about 80 ms

USB transfer plus a lower frame rate (24 vs 30 fps) delay the phone's answer. Wi-Fi works but stalls for up to 0.36 s.

An earlier run (Sign Check game, same day, phone at a different angle) found the opposite: Mac 79% → three cameras 89%. Extra cameras only help when they see the hand from a different angle. In today's sessions the phone and the Mac both faced the hand head-on, so they made the same mistakes.

The setups compared

Recognised: the right sign shown on 2 frames in a row within 2 s of the cue. Latency: from the cue to the right sign being ready on the Mac. It includes the person's own reaction (~0.6–0.8 s), which is the same for every setup. +Delay: latency minus the fastest of Mac / phone pair / all-3 on the same cue, so the reaction cancels out. Settled: share of frames showing the right sign from 1 s after the cue until the next cue. vs Mac: paired bootstrap over the 90 cues (2,000 resamples).

Accuracy by sign

Speed

Processing is the time from a frame being ready on the Mac to the hand result (MediaPipe on the Mac, 640 px wide). Phone frames also spend time being captured, JPEG-encoded and sent over USB before this.

Where the errors come from

  • THUMBS UP → FIST. Seen from the front, a raised thumb lies over the curled fingers, so the thumb's joint angles look bent. The phone (lower, also frontal) sees this even worse than the Mac.
  • PEACE → HORNS about 8% of the time on all setups. The ring and pinky are half-curled in a relaxed peace sign, and the template is unsure.
  • FIST → THUMBS UP on the Mac (11%): the opposite thumb error. The phone is better here (91% vs 86%).
  • Learned trust per finger (share of settled frames where the finger was read right): thumb Mac 73%, phone 56%. Index to pinky: both 96–99%. The thumb is the one finger where the views differ, and here the Mac sees it better.

How the three cameras were combined

The live version (all3_live) used per-finger trust learned in the earlier game, where the phone was placed better and saw the thumb better. Here that trust was out of date: it took the phone's thumb and lost 6 points. Learning trust from the other sessions of the same placement (all3_cv) fixed it. Trust has to be re-learned whenever a camera moves.

Connection: Wi-Fi vs USB

Same phone app, 960×720 JPEG pairs at quality 70. Wi-Fi is 5 GHz at 433 Mbps, signal −35 dBm. Both carry about 23 fps. USB is steadier: its worst gap is 5× shorter, which matters for push-to-press and fast signs.

Depth for Air Buttons

Pushing toward the screen is measured by how much bigger the hand looks to the Mac camera, and optionally by the 1 cm stereo between the phone lenses. The stereo shift is only 2.5–3 px for a full push, too small to rely on: alone it gave 12 false presses in 4 hover tasks. Mac hand scale is the usable depth signal. Navigation still needs work: half of the pushes landed and hovering often pressed by accident.

Benefits and drawbacks of each setup

Mac camera alone 94% · 85%

Benefits
  • Most accurate and fastest in this study
  • Nothing to set up: no phone, cable, pairing or Shizuku
  • 30 fps, about 22 ms processing
  • Lowest CPU and battery use
Drawbacks
  • One viewpoint: when the thumb or a finger is hidden, nothing recovers it
  • Depth only from hand size, which drifts with tilt and distance
  • Fixed in the laptop lid, so it can't look from the side
Best for
  • Everyday gesture control, slide control, demos

Phone, 2 cameras 89% · 78%

Benefits
  • Can be placed anywhere: above the desk, at the side, facing a tabletop
  • Two lenses at once give a small stereo depth near the lens
  • Better than the Mac at FIST and HORNS
  • Frees the laptop camera for video calls
Drawbacks
  • About 80 ms slower, 24 fps
  • Very poor on THUMBS UP (9%) from a low, frontal angle
  • The two lenses are 1 cm apart, so they mostly make the same mistakes
  • Needs the app, Shizuku, pairing, and the screen kept on
Best for
  • Tabletop and touch work (StickyBeat, mat piano) and mounts away from the screen

All 3 cameras 94% · 85%

Benefits
  • Never worse than the best camera, once trust is learned for the placement
  • Gained 10 points (79 → 89%) when the phone saw from a different angle
  • Keeps working if one view loses the hand
  • Records multi-view data for research
Drawbacks
  • No gain when the cameras share a viewpoint (this study)
  • Stale trust hurts (−6 points): it must be re-learned after moving a camera
  • Most setup and most CPU (3 trackers)
  • Waits for the phone's frames
Best for
  • Hard signs and occlusion, with the phone placed at 45–90° to the Mac

Method

  1. The screen cues one of six signs (FIST, OPEN PALM, PEACE, POINT, THUMBS UP, HORNS) every 2.2–3.2 s, in random blocks of six, 30 cues per session.
  2. The Mac camera (1280×720) and the phone's main and ultra-wide lenses (960×720, logical camera 4, streamed over USB) are tracked separately with MediaPipe. Every frame's finger probabilities, landmarks, size and brightness are saved.
  3. Each setup is rebuilt offline on a 20 ms grid from each camera's latest frame (≤150 ms old), with no smoothing, then scored against the cues (research.py).
  4. Signs are classified from the joint angles of each finger, matched to a template per sign.

Limits

  • One participant, one room, daylight, one phone placement (low, frontal). The results show this placement, not every placement.
  • Three sessions: the spread between sessions (±4.5 points on settled accuracy) is as large as most differences between setups.
  • Latency includes human reaction, so only the +delay differences compare the systems.

Next experiments

  • Same test with the phone at 45° and 90° to the side, to measure how much a different viewpoint adds.
  • More people (5–8) for between-person variation.
  • A better thumb feature (thumb tip vs index base direction) before adding more cameras.
  • Low-light session, to test whether the phone's sensor helps where the Mac camera gets noisy.
