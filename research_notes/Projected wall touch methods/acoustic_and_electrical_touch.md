# Non-camera touch sensing for a projected wall (acoustic, vibration, ultrasound, electrical) and fusion with a fingertip camera

Context: projector on an ordinary painted wall, Mac host (Python/Swift), student budget in India, Nov 2026. A camera already gives accurate fingertip x,y. Fusion model: **camera = where, sensor = when** (contact onset, hold, release).

**Scope update (from user):** hardware should be ONLY the POCO M4 Pro (Android), the projector and the Mac, with no extra sensors if possible. Sections 0a-0c cover the phone and MacBook as the "when" sensor and are the priority. Sections 1-5 are background, and the conductive-paint / IR-frame / piezo material in sections 3-4 is the **"cheap add-on" tier**.

## 0a. Can the phone's own mic/IMU (resting on or taped to the wall) or the MacBook mics detect a fingertip touch on a painted wall? Papers on phone-on-surface tap sensing

### Takeaway
Yes for tap onset, with caveats. Several papers use only a phone's built-in microphones or IMU, coupled to the surface, to detect and even localise taps. They work on *tables* (wood), with the phone lying on the same surface. None reports results on a painted masonry wall. A phone **taped flat to the wall within roughly 0.5-1 m of the touch area** and recording the **microphone** (48 kHz, structure-borne pickup through the chassis) is the most promising zero-extra-hardware "when" sensor. The accelerometer is limited to about 200 Hz on Android 12+ without a permission (400-500 Hz typical with it), which is enough for a firm tap but weak for soft touches. The MacBook's airborne mics will probably not hear a soft fingertip on a wall (inference). Fingertip *pad* contact is the hardest case. Knuckle, nail and fingertip taps are distinct acoustic events (TapSense).

### Cited Findings
- **Toffee (Xiao et al., MobileHCI 2014):** a device resting on a hard table, acoustically coupled by gravity alone, senses taps on the table around it through acoustic TDOA. Bearing error is 4.3° (laptop prototype). It is the canonical "the device itself is the sensor" paper, though it localises direction rather than x,y. — [Robert Xiao Toffee page](https://www.robertxiao.ca/research/toffee/); [chrisharrison.net](https://chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/Toffee)
- **UbiK (Wang, Zhao, Zhang, Peng, MobiSys 2014, pp. 14-27):** uses a phone's **two built-in microphones** to localise keystrokes on a paper keyboard on a table, exploiting multipath fading. It reports above 95% key localisation accuracy (self-reported) and faster text entry than on-screen keyboards in a field trial. It needs per-key training taps. — [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/mobisys/WangZZP14.html); [paper PDF (typeset.io)](https://typeset.io/pdf/ubiquitous-keyboard-for-small-mobile-devices-harnessing-3dd2sxxk1e.pdf); [MobiSys epub](https://old.sigmobile.org/mobisys/2014/epubMainConference/sys217-zhangA.epub)
- **VibSense (Liu, Chen, Gruteser, Wang):** a *single* piezo vibration sensor with an op-amp, plugged into a phone's **audio jack** so the phone's audio ADC (48 kHz or more) samples it. On a 120×50×3 cm wooden table, finger "clicks" at nearby keys (about 2 cm apart) were recognised at about 87% (3 training taps/key) and 97% (5 taps/key) by PSD fingerprinting. Overall accuracy is above 95% across use cases. The authors say finger clicks contain a broad frequency range and short duration, and that piezo has the widest frequency response and lowest cost compared with geophones and MEMS accelerometers. This needs one tiny add-on sensor, but the phone does all the processing. — [VibSense PDF (Stevens)](https://personal.stevens.edu/~ychen6/papers/VibSense%20Sensing%20Touches%20on%20Ubiquitous%20Surfaces%20through%20Vibration.pdf); [Rutgers version](https://www.winlab.rutgers.edu/~gruteser/papers/p507-liu.pdf)
- **Vibrative:** used a phone's 3-axis accelerometer to classify taps on a table by vibration strength and frequency. It is described as a proof of concept that worked better on small surfaces (secondhand news source). — [search result summary, original not retrieved]. *Treat as weak evidence.*
- **Knocker (KAIST; CHI 2018 EA → IMWUT/UbiComp 2019):** knocking on an object *with the phone held against it* and fusing the phone's microphone, accelerometer and gyroscope identifies objects at 98% (23 objects, lab) and 83% (in the wild, noisy). This shows that phone mic + IMU together capture solid-borne knock responses well. — [KAIST NMSL Knocker project](https://nmsl.kaist.ac.kr/projects/knocker); [KAIST repository](https://koasas.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/249673); [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2019-10-identification-interaction-smartphone.html)
- **ViBand (Laput, Xiao, Harrison, UIST 2016, Best Paper):** a custom smartwatch kernel raised the commodity accelerometer from about 100 Hz to **4 kHz**, turning it into a contact "vibration microphone" that hears taps on the arm/hand and grasped-tool vibrations, and is largely immune to airborne noise. The key lesson is that IMU sample rate, not sensitivity, limits tap sensing via stock APIs. — [Robert Xiao ViBand](https://www.robertxiao.ca/research/viband/); [figlab ViBand](https://www.figlab.com/research/2016/viband); [paper PDF](https://modernmobile.cs.washington.edu/docs/viband.pdf)
- **Android IMU rate cap:** from Android 12 (API 31), apps targeting SDK 31+ are throttled to **200 Hz** for accelerometer, gyro and magnetometer unless they declare `android.permission.HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS`. This is a normal install-time permission. Debug builds requesting more than 200 Hz without it throw a SecurityException. — [Expo Sensors docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v54.0.0/sdk/sensors); [AOSP change (e/OS mirror)](https://gitlab.e.foundation/e/os/android_frameworks_base/-/commit/3e244be4e99944b709eeaa9504ee2150338a159a)
- **TapSense (Harrison, Schwarz, Hudson, UIST 2011):** the impact sound tells fingertip, pad, nail and knuckle apart. Four-class accuracy is about 95%, and two-class (for example finger vs pen) is above 99%. The CMU release notes that stock phone microphones are designed for voice and "would not work well" for this. TapSense used a dedicated contact mic, later commercialised by Qeexo as FingerSense. — [chrisharrison.net TapSense](https://chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/TapSense); [CMU news](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2011/october/oct19_tapsense.html)
- **ScratchInput (Harrison & Hudson, UIST 2008):** dragging a fingernail across textured surfaces, explicitly including **wall paint**, makes sound that carries several metres through dense materials. Six gestures were recognised at about 90%, but with no location. This is the strongest evidence that *continuous moving contact* on a painted wall is acoustically audible, though it needs a nail, a textured surface and a contact pickup. — [chrisharrison.net ScratchInput](https://www.chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/ScratchInput); [arXiv 2111.15053 (deep-learning follow-up)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.15053)
- **SurfaceVibe (Pan et al., IPSN 2017):** four geophones on a surface track taps (with dispersion and reflection modelling) **and swipes**, segmenting a swipe into "slip pulses" and tracking the trajectory. Accuracy is cm-level. MM-Tap (IEEE IoT J 2023) claims mm-level with three geophones. This is evidence that dragging produces trackable vibration on rigid surfaces. — [AMiner SurfaceVibe](https://www.aminer.cn/pub/599c7b3e601a182cd271dc45/surfacevibe-vibration-based-tap-swipe-tracking-on-ubiquitous-surfaces); [MM-Tap PDF](https://island520.github.io/pdf/MM-Tap.pdf); [survey arXiv 2512.05071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.05071)
- **SAWSense (CHI 2023):** argues that accelerometers and piezos lack the bandwidth to separate swipe from scratch, and uses bone-conduction voice-pickup units instead. Tap/swipe/scratch accuracy is above 97%. — [U-Michigan news](https://news.umich.edu/?p=140623); [Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/news/take-a-swipe-at-it-0ab40a59e5db)
- **Material matters:** a contact mic got strong signals on wood and particleboard, moderate on metal and faint on glass. — [US patent 12474831](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12474831)
- **MIT knock tracker:** hand (as opposed to knuckle) impacts are much harder because of dispersive bending waves and variable transients. — [MIT paper PDF](https://aspirin.media.mit.edu/responsive/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2013/12/Passive-Acoustic-Knock-Tracking-for-Interactive-Windows.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Phone mic vs phone accelerometer:** the mic samples at 48 kHz, and a phone pressed or taped flat against the wall picks up structure-borne sound through its chassis. The accelerometer at 200-500 Hz only catches the low-frequency thump. For a *soft fingertip pad* on painted plaster or brick the energy is small and mostly low frequency, so expect: knuckle or nail tap reliably detected; firm fingertip tap usually detected within about 0.5-1 m; light "touch-screen-style" contact often missed. The user should test this empirically. No paper measures it.
- **Coupling is everything.** Phone flat against the wall (double-sided foam tape, or a rubber band/strap). A phone lying on a table that touches the wall is a much weaker path because energy is lost at the wall-table joint.
- **Turn off phone audio processing:** use `AudioSource.UNPROCESSED` (or VOICE_RECOGNITION if UNPROCESSED is unsupported) so AGC, noise suppression and the voice high-pass don't eat taps. This follows from the TapSense note that phone mics are tuned for voice. The UNPROCESSED behaviour on the POCO M4 Pro is unverified.
- **MacBook mics:** they hear only airborne sound. A knock on the wall is audible, but a soft fingertip touch on paint makes almost no airborne sound and the projector fan masks it. They are usable only for knocks or as a noise reference to reject room sounds (subtract or flag events that are loud on the MacBook but weak on the wall-coupled phone).
- **Continuous contact / drawing:** acoustics detect touch-down (impact) reliably, and drag only through friction noise, which is stronger with nails or textured paint (ScratchInput, SurfaceVibe slip pulses) and weak with a soft fingertip on smooth paint. A stationary hold is silent (the Elo hold limitation in section 1). Practical design: the **audio onset starts a stroke** and the **camera keeps it alive** (fingertip stays within a depth/shadow tolerance of the wall; lift-off when the fingertip leaves). Optionally use friction-noise energy as a "still in contact" confidence boost.
- **Localisation from one phone is unnecessary** because the camera gives x,y. The phone's two mics (UbiK) could add a coarse left/right sanity check to reject wall impacts far from the tracked fingertip.

### Gaps
- No paper found measuring phone-mic or phone-IMU detection of soft fingertip taps on a **painted masonry or plaster wall**. All phone-on-surface results are on tables.
- The POCO M4 Pro's maximum accelerometer rate with HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS, and whether it supports `UNPROCESSED` audio, were not found.
- TapLoc and VibWrite: no sources found under these names.
- The Vibrative primary paper was not retrieved.

## 0b. Code / GitHub repos usable with phone + Mac

### Takeaway
There is no ready-made "phone mic onset + camera fingertip" repo. Usable building blocks: ALTo's simple pyaudio threshold-plus-sample-difference code design, cansik/sonic-touch-localization, TapTDOA, FIGLAB/ubicoustics (Python sound-event classification), and Google Oboe/AAudio for low-latency Android capture.

### Cited Findings
- **ALTo (2021):** Python + pyaudio at 44.1 kHz, 2-channel. An onset is a sample amplitude above 1000 (16-bit). TDOA comes from the sample-index difference ÷ sample rate. Accuracy is 1.45 cm (x) and 2.72 cm (y). The authors note that dropped audio samples occurred when processing was too slow. This is a good minimal template. — [arXiv 2108.06837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06837)
- **[cansik/sonic-touch-localization](https://github.com/cansik/sonic-touch-localization):** tabletop multi-mic TDOA with threshold onset. — [ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fcansik%2Fsonic-touch-localization)
- **TapTDOA:** piezos plus filtered cross-correlation for precise time offsets. — [Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/159821-taptdoa); [Hackster](https://blog.hackster.io/taptdoa-turns-any-hard-surface-into-a-control-interface-bc161740981a)
- **Single-piezo CNN tap localisation (Python recording scripts):** — [mylonasc blog](https://mylonasc.github.io/2019-10-06-scal)
- **[FIGLAB/ubicoustics](https://github.com/FIGLAB/ubicoustics):** Python 3 + TensorFlow/Keras sound-event recogniser with live-prediction scripts. It reached about 80% on activities (it is for activity sound, not taps). It is a template for classifying "tap vs knock vs noise". A commercial licence goes via CMU. — [paper PDF](https://mynkgoel.github.io/pdfs/ubicoustics.pdf)
- **Oboe** (Google C++ library): uses AAudio on Android 8.1+ and OpenSL ES below, aiming for the lowest possible latency per device. — [Android Oboe docs](https://developer.android.com/games/sdk/oboe); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2018/11/android-oboe)

### Inferences
- The simplest prototype needs no app build: an existing Android "phone as USB/Wi-Fi microphone" app, or `adb` + scrcpy audio forwarding, makes the phone mic appear on the Mac, and Python `sounddevice` runs onset detection there. This adds unknown and variable latency, so it is fine for proving detectability and not for final timing (inference; specific apps not verified).
- Better approach: a small Android app (Kotlin, AudioRecord or Oboe, with optional SensorManager at the highest rate) does onset detection **on the phone** and sends only event packets `{t_onset_boottime_ns, energy, centroid}` over UDP to the Mac. This avoids streaming-audio latency.

### Gaps
- No GitHub project found that fuses a fingertip camera with phone audio/IMU tap onsets.
- No Android→Mac low-latency audio-over-UDP repo verified.

## 0c. Time-syncing phone audio/IMU events with the Mac camera

### Takeaway
Android gives capture-side timestamps for both audio (`AudioRecord.getTimestamp`, API 24+, MONOTONIC or BOOTTIME timebase, measured "at the earliest point available in the capture pipeline") and sensors (`SensorEvent.timestamp`, ns). Estimate the phone-to-Mac clock offset with an NTP-style UDP ping (a few ms of error over Wi-Fi), then calibrate a constant audio-vs-camera lag once using deliberate taps.

### Cited Findings
- `AudioRecord.getTimestamp(AudioTimestamp, timebase)`, added in API 24: "The AudioTimestamp reflects the frame delivery information at the earliest point available in the capture pipeline". The timebase is `TIMEBASE_MONOTONIC` or `TIMEBASE_BOOTTIME`. The frame count resets to 0 after stop()/startRecording(). — [Android AudioRecord reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/AudioRecord#getTimestamp(android.media.AudioTimestamp,%20int))
- On the Mac side, `sounddevice` callbacks expose `inputBufferAdcTime` (see section 5). — [python-sounddevice](https://python-sounddevice.readthedocs.io/api/streams.html)

### Inferences
- Onset time on the phone = `ts.nanoTime + (onsetFrame − ts.framePosition)/fs`. Send it to the Mac, convert with the ping-estimated offset (take the minimum-RTT sample of about 20 pings and refresh every few seconds), then match it to the camera frame timestamp.
- Wi-Fi jitter affects only *delivery* time, not the timestamp, so a 30-60 fps camera with a ±5-10 ms sync error still picks the correct frame or interpolates the fingertip. For the "feels instant" path, fire the touch on packet arrival using the latest fingertip position. Expected end-to-end latency is roughly phone buffer 5-20 ms + onset 1-5 ms + Wi-Fi 5-20 ms, which plausibly stays under 50 ms (unmeasured).
- `SensorEvent.timestamp` (elapsedRealtimeNanos timebase on modern devices) can be fused with the audio onset on the phone (both peaks within a few ms means a real wall tap; audio only means probably airborne noise). This mirrors Knocker's mic + IMU fusion. *(The SensorEvent timebase detail is from general Android knowledge and was not re-verified here.)*

### Gaps
- No measured Wi-Fi phone↔Mac sync accuracy or POCO audio input latency found.

## 1. Acoustic tap localisation / TDOA with piezo contact mics (Toffee, TAI-CHI, Paradiso knock tracking, Elo APR, ScratchInput, Lamb waves)

### Takeaway
Passive acoustic TDOA reaches roughly 1.5-4 cm accuracy on hard, uniform plates (glass, tables) at ~65 ms latency, but it works best on *knocks/taps*, sees only the impact transient, and cannot sense a held finger or a drag. On plaster or brick, published accuracy figures for soft fingertip taps were not found. For fusion that does not matter much: piezos only need to give a reliable "contact happened at time t" spike, and the camera supplies the position.

### Cited Findings
- **Paradiso et al., MIT Media Lab ("Passive Acoustic Knock Tracking for Interactive Windows", CHI 2002 EA / IEEE Sensors 2002):** four contact piezo pickups near the corners of a glass sheet. A DSP estimates position from differential timing, amplitude and frequency content. Resolution is about σ = 2 cm across 1.5 m of 1/4" tempered glass. — [MIT Tapper page](https://resenv.media.mit.edu/Tapper/); [paper PDF](https://aspirin.media.mit.edu/responsive/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2013/12/Passive-Acoustic-Knock-Tracking-for-Interactive-Windows.pdf)
- Paradiso & Leo (Sensor Review 2005) report σ = 2.5 cm on 1/4" tempered glass and σ = 3-4 cm on 1 cm shatterproof glass, over areas up to 2×2 m, with **65 ms latency**. — [Sensor Review abstract](https://d2pnhgckm19p0r.cloudfront.net/insight/content/doi/10.1108/02602280510585727/full/html); [Large displays paper](https://aspirin.media.mit.edu/responsive/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2013/12/Passive-Acoustic-Sensing-for-Tracking-Knocks-Atop-Large-Interactive-Displays.pdf)
- The same MIT work says that tracking hand (as opposed to knuckle) hits on glass is "much harder" because bending waves are highly dispersive and transients vary a lot from knock to knock. The system targets knuckle knocks, not fingertip taps. — [paper PDF](https://aspirin.media.mit.edu/responsive/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2013/12/Passive-Acoustic-Knock-Tracking-for-Interactive-Windows.pdf)
- **Toffee (Xiao, Lew, Marsanico, Hariharan, Hudson, Harrison, MobileHCI 2014, pp. 67-76):** acoustic TDOA from sensors in a device resting on a hard tabletop, coupled to it only by gravity. It gives touch *bearing* (angle), with a mean error of 4.3° on a laptop prototype, not a full 2D position. Earlier time-of-arrival systems needed semi-permanent surface instrumentation. — [Robert Xiao project page](https://www.robertxiao.ca/research/toffee/); [chrisharrison.net](https://chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/Toffee)
- **MoBat (2015, TUM):** similar TDOA, about 3 cm average accuracy and precision on a 1 m × 2 m table. — [TUM PDF](https://www.campar.in.tum.de/pub/kreskowski2015mobat/kreskowski2015mobat.pdf)
- **ALTo (2021, arXiv 2108.06837):** four piezo mics on the surface, TDOA plus hyperbolic equations. Accuracy is 1.45 cm in x and 2.72 cm in y. It targets large surfaces "such as a table or even a wall" for motor-impaired users. Its main weakness is detecting impulse onset with an amplitude threshold, which shifts with tap loudness. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06837)
- **TAI-CHI (EU FP6 IST-507882, Tangible Acoustic Interfaces for Computer-Human Interaction):** cheap acoustic pickups on wood, plastic, glass, metal "or even the plaster of a wall". There was a 2006 demo of tapping on a wall in place of a keyboard. Methods studied: TDOA, time-frequency analysis of dispersive velocities, time reversal, Location Template Matching (fingerprinting) and continuous tracking. Plate thickness is the key determinant of resolution. — [Cardiff thesis record](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/54576/); [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/?p=1005); [The Future of Things](https://thefutureofthings.com/3420-tai-chi-future-interactive-interface/)
- TAI-CHI papers note that in-solid wave propagation makes TDOA estimation complex. Achievable location resolution is tied to the mean wavelength of the detected wave, and background noise, reflections and dispersion are the main problems. — [NIME 2005 paper](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2005/nime2005_097.pdf); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/54576)
- **Elo Acoustic Pulse Recognition (APR, from Sensitive Object):** four small transducers on the glass edges. Location comes from matching acoustic "fingerprints" (amplitude/phase profiles) against a pre-recorded table. — [Elo APR datasheet](https://www.touchwindow.com/mm5/graphics/00000001/elo/apr_ds.pdf); [touchwindow APR](https://touchwindow.com/c/Acoustic-Pulse-Recognition.html)
- APR **cannot detect a stationary hold.** An Elo patent says conventional bending-wave systems cannot identify a hold condition, and that acoustic methods are limited at detecting prolonged holds and touch release. Elo's workarounds use low-frequency components or add a capacitive layer that detects lift-off. Drag works because a moving finger makes friction noise. — [US8659579B2](https://www.google.co.uk/patents/US8659579); [Justia 20110025649](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20110025649); [Kiosk Marketplace](https://www.kioskmarketplace.com/news/inside-elos-new-apr-touchscreen-technology-2)
- **ScratchInput (Harrison & Hudson, UIST 2008):** a modified stethoscope plus mic picks up fingernail-scratch sound on textured surfaces, including wall paint, over several metres through dense material. Six gestures were recognised at about 90% accuracy (89.5%, 15 participants). It **cannot localise** the input. — [chrisharrison.net ScratchInput](https://www.chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/ScratchInput); [arXiv 2111.15053](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.15053); [Wired](https://wirednews.pages.dev/2009/08/scratch-input)
- A patent on surface-acoustic gesture sensing reports that a contact mic got strong signals on wood and particleboard, moderate on metal and faint on glass, so the material matters a lot. — [US patent 12474831](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12474831)
- A tap-location classification patent notes that accuracy often drops when the surface or tap type varies. — [search summary via uspto.report](https://uspto.report/patent/app/20150242036)

### Inferences
- On a plaster or brick wall a fingertip pad tap (soft, low energy, absorbed by a lossy, inhomogeneous material) will give a much weaker and lower-frequency transient than a knuckle knock on glass. Expect to need piezos close to the touch zone, for example a cluster within about 0.5-1 m of the projected area, with high preamp gain. Knocks will be easy and soft taps marginal. This is inference: no measured wall/fingertip numbers were found.
- For fusion, localisation is unnecessary. One or two piezos give a timestamped "impact" event, and the camera's fingertip position at that timestamp is the touch point. Multiple piezos can still help reject impacts far from the camera's fingertip (a coarse consistency check).
- Passive acoustics detect *onset* only. For drag or drawing the sensor sees nothing while the finger is still and only faint friction noise while it moves, so "contact held" must come from elsewhere: camera heuristics, capacitive, or the APR-style hybrid.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy for soft fingertip taps on painted plaster, brick or drywall was found (the TAI-CHI thesis may contain some but was not read in full).
- Toffee's absolute cm error and latency were not available (only the 4.3° bearing error).
- No Lamb-wave-specific wall paper was retrieved beyond the TAI-CHI dispersion discussion.

## 2. Ultrasound / active acoustic and vibration sensing (SAWSense, Vibrosight, SurfaceSight, Acoustruments, active sonar)

### Takeaway
Active and surface-wave methods mostly do event *classification* (tap vs swipe vs scratch, or activity recognition), not precise wall touch tracking. The most fusion-relevant one is SAWSense: bone-conduction voice-pickup (VPU) sensors capture surface waves with high bandwidth and reject airborne noise, and taps, scratches and swipes are classified at better than 97% accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- **SAWSense (Iravantchi, Zhao, Kin, Sample, CHI 2023, Best Paper):** voice pickup units (bone-conduction mics) detect only surface-borne acoustic waves. Their sealed housing makes them insensitive to ambient noise. Taps, scratches and swipes, desk trackpad-style gestures and 16 kitchen activities were recognised at better than 97% average accuracy with a Random Forest. The authors argue accelerometers and piezos lack the bandwidth to tell a swipe from a scratch. — [U-Michigan news](https://news.umich.edu/?p=140623); [Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/news/take-a-swipe-at-it-0ab40a59e5db); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/science/sawsense-surface-acoustic-waves-interface)
- **Vibrosight (Zhang, Laput, Harrison, UIST 2018):** long-range laser vibrometry of a single point via a retro-reflective tag, for activity sensing. It avoids microphone interference. It is an activity/event detector, not touch tracking. — [chrisharrison.net Vibrosight](https://chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/Vibrosight); [Hackaday on Vibrosight++](https://hackaday.com/2021/05/21/with-a-big-enough-laser-the-world-is-your-sensor)
- **SurfaceSight (Laput & Harrison, CHI 2019):** a LIDAR sweep just above a surface for object and touch sensing around a smart speaker. No accuracy figures were retrieved. — [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/LaputH19/bibtex); [MicroVision blog](https://microvision.blogspot.com/2019/06/this-lidar-smart-speaker-imagines-alexa.html)
- A related optical, non-acoustic result: EclipseTouch (Mollyn, DeVrio, Harrison, 2025) uses IR shadow casting and reports 98.0% touch-contact accuracy and 6.9 mm hover error. It is camera-based, so it is outside this note's scope but a relevant competitor. — [arXiv 2509.03430](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03430v1)

### Inferences
- A VPU or bone-conduction sensor glued to the wall is a plausible "when" detector that is more robust to room noise (projector fan, speech) than a bare piezo. Unverified on walls.
- Acoustruments (Laput et al., CHI 2015) works on phone-attached acoustic structures, not walls. It is not applicable here.

### Gaps
- No active-ultrasound (sonar or swept-sine through the wall) touch-tracking system for walls was found with numbers.
- SAWSense latency and the exact sensor part number were not retrieved.

## 3. Electrical / capacitive surfaces: Electrick, Wall++, Touché, Bare Conductive / MPR121, PCAP foil, IR touch frames

### Takeaway
These are the only non-camera options here that sense **continuous contact** (hold and drag), which acoustics cannot. Wall++ (conductive nickel paint under latex, about US$20/m²) and Electrick (EFT, about 1 cm accuracy) prove a painted wall can be a touch sensor. The easiest student build is an MPR121 (about ₹75) driving a few conductive-paint electrode zones as a "finger is touching" signal, with the camera giving position. An off-the-shelf IR touch frame (about ₹10,000 for 65" in India) gives full x,y plus hold/drag on any flat wall, but it actually detects beam breaks just above the surface, not true contact.

### Cited Findings
- **Electrick (Zhang, Laput, Harrison, CHI 2017):** electric field tomography. Small currents are injected between electrode pairs on the perimeter of a conductive coating (spray paint, bulk plastic, carbon-loaded film), and voltage differences locate the finger. Touch location accuracy is about **1 cm**, "sufficient for buttons and sliders" but less precise than conventional touchscreens. It works on large or irregular surfaces and takes a protective topcoat. — [CMU news](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2017/may/touchpads-with-spray-paint.html); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/05/170508112328.htm); [figlab Electrick](https://www.figlab.com/research/2017/electrick)
- **Wall++ (Zhang, Yang, Hudson, Harrison, Sample, CHI 2018, CMU + Disney Research):** two coats of conductive (nickel-based, cheaper than silver) paint rolled on in a diamond grid pattern, hidden under a standard latex topcoat, applied with rollers, brushes and painter's tape. Materials cost about **US$20/m²** at retail. — [figlab Wall++](https://www.figlab.com/research/2018/wall); [CMU news](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2018/april/paint-transforms-walls-into-sensors.html); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/this-conductive-paint-turns-walls-into-giant-touchscreens-180968922/)

### Inferences
- **IR frame on a painted wall:** the frame mounts flat on the wall around the projected area and connects over USB HID (it appears as a touchscreen/mouse). It detects any object that breaks the IR grid a few mm off the surface, so hover near the wall can register as a touch. It gives hold and drag natively. It is the quickest "just works" option but stops being a research contribution, and the projector image must fit inside the frame size.
- **PCAP foil** needs a non-conductive substrate (glass/acrylic) and is designed to sense *through* glass. Sticking it on a painted wall might work through a thin acrylic sheet, but this is unverified, and a wall containing moisture or wiring could detune it.
- **DIY capacitive "contact" layer:** paint a few large conductive stripes or zones (Bare Conductive or carbon/nickel paint) under the wall paint and connect each to an MPR121 electrode. Mutual or self capacitance then gives *touch on/off and held contact* per zone. The camera gives the exact point, and the active zone confirms contact, so dragging works because contact stays asserted. With Wall++-style diamond rows and columns plus a proper mutual-cap controller, coarse x,y is also possible. MPR121 is designed for small electrodes, so large painted zones may need sensitivity and threshold tuning (inference).

## 5. Time-sync with the camera on a Mac, latency budget, and code

### Takeaway
Use hardware timestamps on both streams. `sounddevice` callbacks expose the ADC capture time of the first sample (`time.inputBufferAdcTime`), and AVFoundation/camera frames carry presentation timestamps. Map both to one host clock, detect the onset in audio, then select the camera fingertip at that time (interpolated, or the next frame). Audio onset detection itself adds only a few ms. The latency budget is dominated by camera frame interval and exposure (about 16-33 ms) plus processing, so under 50 ms is plausible.

### Cited Findings
- `sounddevice` stream callbacks receive a time struct with `inputBufferAdcTime` (ADC capture time of the first input sample), `outputBufferDacTime` and `currentTime`, all in the same time base as `Stream.time`. — [python-sounddevice Streams docs](https://python-sounddevice.readthedocs.io/api/streams.html)
- `latency='low'` gives interactive latency. A non-zero `blocksize` can add an extra buffering layer on some host APIs. Callbacks must not allocate, block or do file I/O. — [python-sounddevice Streams docs](https://python-sounddevice.readthedocs.io/api/streams.html)
- On macOS, recordings can come back silent if the host app/terminal lacks microphone permission (noted in the SoundCard library docs). — [SoundCard on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/SoundCard/)
- Reference latency: the MIT knock tracker gave coordinates in about 65 ms. — [MIT Tapper](https://resenv.media.mit.edu/Tapper/)
- **Open-source projects:**
  - [cansik/sonic-touch-localization](https://github.com/cansik/sonic-touch-localization): multi-mic tabletop TDOA with threshold onset ([ecosyste.ms listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fcansik%2Fsonic-touch-localization)).
  - TapTDOA (Ben Hencke): cheap piezos, filtered and cross-correlated waveforms for precise time offsets ([Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/159821-taptdoa); [Hackster](https://blog.hackster.io/taptdoa-turns-any-hard-surface-into-a-control-interface-bc161740981a)).
  - Single-channel piezo tap localisation with deep CNNs, with Python recording scripts ([mylonasc blog](https://mylonasc.github.io/2019-10-06-scal)).
- Reju et al. (IEEE Trans. Multimedia 2013): TDOA estimation for taps on solid surfaces, verified on aluminium and glass. — [NTU record](https://nanyangtechnologicaluniv.demo.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/localization-of-taps-on-solid-surfaces-for-human-computer-touch-i)

### Inferences
- Suggested pipeline (Python):
  1. A `sounddevice.InputStream` at 48 kHz with a small block (64-256 frames, about 1.3-5 ms) pushes blocks plus `inputBufferAdcTime` into a ring buffer.
  2. Onset detection uses a high-pass filter (about 100-200 Hz, to remove room rumble), then short-window energy or spectral flux with an adaptive noise floor (`librosa.onset.onset_strength` works offline, but a hand-rolled envelope detector is lower latency for live use).
  3. Onset sample time = `adc_time + idx/fs`.
  4. Camera frames get timestamps from AVFoundation `CMSampleBuffer` presentation time (Swift) or a host clock (Python/OpenCV, less accurate).
  5. Calibrate the fixed audio-vs-camera offset once by tapping repeatedly and maximising agreement with the camera's fingertip-Z-stop moment.
  6. Emit touch-down at the camera x,y nearest that time.
- **Hold and drag:** acoustics give only touch-down (and maybe friction noise while moving). Keep "contact" asserted while the camera fingertip stays within a depth/shadow tolerance of the wall, or add a capacitive zone (MPR121) that reports true contact for down, hold and up. That is the Elo acoustic + capacitive hybrid pattern.
- **Soft-tap vs knock:** use the onset energy and spectral centroid as a feature. Set the threshold from a calibration session on the actual wall, and require camera agreement (fingertip near the wall at that time) to reject false triggers from footsteps or door slams.
- **Latency budget (rough inference):** audio buffering about 3-10 ms, onset detection about 1-5 ms, camera frame wait up to 1 frame (17 ms at 60 fps, 33 ms at 30 fps), processing plus app about 5-10 ms. That totals roughly 25-55 ms, so under 50 ms needs a 60 fps camera or emitting the event on the audio onset using the last known fingertip position.

### Gaps
- No source was found specifying how `sounddevice`/PortAudio blocksize maps to CoreAudio buffer sizes on macOS, or for measured Mac round-trip input latency with the UMC404HD.
- No open-source project fusing camera fingertip tracking with piezo onset timing was found.
