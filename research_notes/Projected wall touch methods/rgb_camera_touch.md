# RGB-camera (no depth, no laser) touch vs hover on projected surfaces

Context: the SurfaceTouch tests found fingertip POSITION good (~0.6% of width) but touch-vs-hover near chance with (a) shadow-gap features from a phone near the projector, (b) 1 cm phone stereo, (c) phone + MacBook two-view triangulation (~4 cm depth noise), (d) a side-angle phone view. These notes collect what the literature reports and what geometry it implies.

## 1. Shadow-based touch (PlayAnywhere, Visual Touchpad, Tokyo Denki VIRTOS / Everywhere Switch, Hitachi two-shadow patents)

### Takeaway
Every shadow-touch system that works puts the light source (IR LED or the projector) at a clearly different angle from the camera, so a hovering finger's shadow shows up beside it in the image. The best single-camera result, PlayAnywhere, used a dedicated IR illuminant off the camera axis and got about 3–4 mm touch precision. With a camera close to the projector, the shadow sits mostly behind the finger, and the shadow cue falls apart. That fits the failure in the user's tests.

### Cited Findings
- PlayAnywhere (Wilson, UIST 2005, pp. 83–92) does touch detection with one IR-pass camera and an IR LED illuminant. The projector and camera sit off to the side of the surface, not above it. The paper says that otherwise "true" touch detection needs two cameras — [PlayAnywhere PDF (scispace/typeset)](https://typeset.io/pdf/playanywhere-a-compact-interactive-tabletop-projection-nmgkpegfwe.pdf); [ACM DOI](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F1095034.1095047)
- The PlayAnywhere patent (US7599561) says the IR illuminant is "positioned off axis from the single camera." As the finger approaches, finger and shadow "come together," and at contact the finger hides the shadow completely. Touch is decided by thresholding the width of the shadow just below its topmost point. Candidate fingers are shadows that enter from the bottom of the image. — [US7599561B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7599561B2/en)
- The same patent gives touch location precision of about 3–4 mm (~4.5 image pixels at 640×480), judged subjectively with grating charts, and says height and touch accuracy should be roughly the same. It suggests hover height *could* be computed by matching finger and shadow. That is a proposal, not a measured result. Limits: the surface must reflect IR, the illuminant must be much brighter than stray IR, and dark or pointy objects cause false positives — [US7599561B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7599561B2/en)
- Agarwal et al. (2007) say PlayAnywhere's shadow method works well on opaque projection surfaces "when a finger is pointing in a direction almost perpendicular to that of the infrared light source." They found shadow cues unreliable without directional lighting, with occlusion, and with lighting changes — [Agarwal et al. Tabletop 2007 PDF](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf)
- Visual Touchpad (Malik & Laszlo, ICMI 2004) uses two downward-facing webcams at 320×240, 30 fps, "placed with a sufficiently wide baseline for accurate depth estimation." Touch is a disparity threshold, calibrated by holding the finger about 1 cm above the pad as the "off" height. So its effective touch resolution is about 1 cm — [Malik & Laszlo PDF](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~jflaszlo/papers/icmi-pui-2004/malik_2004_ICMI_visual_touchpad.pdf). Agarwal et al. confirm it "may report a touch event even if a finger is within approximately 1cm from the surface" — [Agarwal 2007](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf)
- Agarwal et al. also describe an earlier single-camera system with "no way to detect contact," which fired mouse events on *pauses* in finger motion (dwell) instead — [Agarwal 2007](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf)
- VIRTOS (Homma & Nakajima, VISAPP 2014) is a plain RGB camera plus projector. The projector is centred in front of the screen and the camera is "at a slant, for example, 50 cm left or right of the center." It works per **button**, not per fingertip. When the shadow fraction inside an 8–12 cm button drops below about 8%, it counts as a touch, because a hovering finger casts a big shadow and a touching finger's shadow nearly disappears. It briefly changes the button colour (green to magenta) to separate shadow pixels from the finger. Shadow segmentation F-measure was 82% at 620 lx and 71% at 12 lx. Button touch failures were 9% (bright, too-fast hand) and 6% (dim, detection failure) for 85 mm buttons — [VIRTOS VISAPP 2014 PDF](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/47286/47286.pdf)
- Everywhere Switch (Nozaki & Nakajima, VISAPP 2015) is a ceiling pan-tilt projector with an RGB camera "slightly offset from the optical axis of the projector (e.g., 50 cm to the side)," about 400 cm from the projection plane, with 7 subjects. It uses the finger/shadow area ratio with hysteresis thresholds. Foreground extraction F > 0.97, but finger-vs-shadow segmentation only F ≈ 0.8 under dim, semi-bright and bright light — [Everywhere Switch VISAPP 2015 PDF](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2015/53076/53076.pdf)
- Hitachi Maxell patents use **two** illuminations from different positions plus one camera. The "approach degree" (proximity) comes from the distance d between matching feature points of the two shadows, and touch is when the two shadow tips meet at the fingertip. IR illuminants and an IR camera keep this separate from the projected image — [US9367176B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9367176); [US10152177B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10152177); [US 9,927,923](https://uspto.report/patent/grant/9,927,923)
- A shadow-geometry patent notes that from one camera, shadow size alone "does not lead to a unique solution to the object's height above a reference surface" without extra constraints — [US9429417](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9429417)
- Newer worn-illuminant shadow systems: ShadowTouch (UIST 2023, wrist-mounted light, camera about 45°) reports a minimum stable touch-up height of about 2 mm — [ShadowTouch ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3586183.3606785) (via search snippet; full text 403). EclipseTouch (UIST 2025, headset camera plus IR emitters casting "structured shadows") reports hover distance mean error of about 6.9 mm and touch contact accuracy of about 98.0% — [arXiv 2509.03430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03430)
- An SIAT paper judges the "fusion level of fingertip and its shadow" in RGB images with a linear model. Hartmann et al. (2012) estimate fingertip height above the surface from shadows. I only saw these in search snippets and could not check the numbers — [SIAT jcjs 201701002](https://jcjs.siat.ac.cn/article/id/201701002)

### Inferences
- **Why the phone near the projector failed.** The shadow lands at about h·tan(α_proj) from the point under the fingertip, and the fingertip appears at about h·tan(α_cam). The visible finger-to-shadow gap is about h·|tan α_proj − tan α_cam| (as vectors on the wall plane). With the camera beside the projector, α_cam ≈ α_proj, so the gap is close to 0 at every height and the shadow hides behind the finger. That is exactly the near-chance result. VIRTOS and Everywhere Switch's 50 cm offset at 400 cm is only about 7°, so they get only a coarse cue and fall back to "shadow area inside a large button," not fingertip-level touch.
- **What is needed:** an angular separation between light and camera of roughly 30–45° or more at the touch point. Example: projector straight-on, camera 0.7–1 m to the side at a 1–1.5 m wall distance. tan 30° ≈ 0.58, so a 1 cm hover gives about 6 mm of gap; tan 45° gives 10 mm. The gap is measured within one image, from finger and shadow at nearly the same spot, so it avoids the cross-camera sync and calibration errors that hurt two-view triangulation.
- The projector's light is the scene content, so on dark or busy projected content the shadow is low-contrast. Every robust system adds its own illuminant (IR LED) or briefly changes the projected colour (VIRTOS). A cheap RGB variant is to have the projector show bright white or a flat colour in a small patch around the tracked fingertip, which gives a clean shadow there.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full PlayAnywhere paper. The PDF would not fetch and MSR's URL returned 404. Exact LED-to-camera angle, hover heights and evaluation numbers come from the patent only.
- I found no published fingertip-level (not button-level) RGB-only shadow touch accuracy with the projector as the only light source.
- Echtler et al. shadow tracking was not found in this session.

## 2. Grazing / side-camera methods (camera nearly parallel to the surface)

### Takeaway
Commercial systems that do this reliably use a light *plane* parallel to the surface, not a camera's view along it: Epson's IR laser curtain, and Sony Xperia Touch's IR plus camera. I found no peer-reviewed RGB-only grazing-camera paper with numbers. Geometrically, though, a grazing view turns hover height directly into an image gap.

### Cited Findings
- Epson finger-touch projectors use a separate Touch Unit with an IR laser curtain just over the board, which detects fingers breaking it. Epson's manual says if the laser "is not emitted parallel to the screen" touch is detected incorrectly, and the surface must be very flat — [Epson EB-595Wi manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/819888/Epson-Eb-595wi.html?page=81); [Epson EB-1485Fi listing](https://kickstartcomputers.com.au/shop/home-theatre/projectors/epson-meetingmate-eb-1485fi-laser-interactive-projector-full-hd-5000-lumens-includes-wall-mount-3yr-wty-v11h919053-free-shipping/)
- Sony Xperia Touch (2017) uses "a combination of Infrared light and real-time detection via its built-in camera" at 60 fps, at about 23 inches projected close to the surface. Reports say touch fails at the largest (80-inch) size because the image is no longer close to the IR sensor — [Sony Xperia Touch coverage](https://maketecheasier.com/sony-xperia-touch-sony-projector); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=1051491)

### Inferences
- The camera's elevation above the wall plane is the key. Viewed at elevation angle β above the wall plane, a fingertip at height h appears about h·cos β above its contact point on the image's "height" axis. The wall's own vanishing line compresses everything else. At β ≈ 10–20° (camera at the wall edge, a few cm out), 1 cm of hover is almost 1 cm of image gap. At the user's "side angle" (probably 45°+ from the wall plane), most of the height cue mixes into in-plane position.
- But grazing views have heavy occlusion (other fingers, the hand, the arm), a narrow strip of usable surface, and strong perspective (far side of the wall only a few pixels tall). They work best for a strip-sized UI near the camera, or with one camera per edge. The near-chance side-angle result was most likely an angle not grazing enough, plus MediaPipe landmark bias at oblique views (see section 6).
- Cheap trick: on a glossy or semi-glossy wall, a grazing camera sees the fingertip's mirror reflection. Touch is when the finger and its reflection meet, which is the same idea as the shadow-tip meeting. This is not sourced; it is a gap to verify.

### Gaps
- I found no academic RGB grazing-camera touch paper with accuracy numbers or a required angle in this session.

## 3. Stereo RGB touch (Agarwal 2007, Visual Touchpad, modern stereo-IR)

### Takeaway
Plain stereo disparity gives roughly cm-level contact sensing. Agarwal et al. got 2–3 mm only by combining many stereo points per fingertip with a learned finger-shape model. Modern stereo-IR (5 cm baseline) gets about 96% contact detection with an ML keypoint model and an IR dot projector. A 1 cm baseline, or two unsynchronised cameras with ~0.5% landmark noise, is far below what is needed.

### Cited Findings
- Agarwal, Izadi, Chandraker & Blake (Tabletop 2007) used two ordinary overhead cameras with a **12 cm baseline, about 50 cm above** the surface. Disparity depth near the surface is quantised every **5 mm**, "so disparity images provide centimetre level precision at best" — [Agarwal 2007 PDF](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf)
- Their algorithm probabilistically aggregates stereo cues from several points per fingertip with a finger-specific geometric model, plus ML tip detection on 8×8 patches. Non-touch was defined as ≥ about 2 mm above the surface, and touch/no-touch accuracy was **98.48%**. Tip-point classification was 92.48%, near 100% after clustering. It ran at up to 20 fps on a 3.4 GHz CPU. Claimed touch precision is **2–3 mm**, against cm-level for earlier stereo systems — [Agarwal 2007 PDF](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf); [MSR page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=159749)
- Goussies et al., "Learning to Detect Touches on Cluttered Tables" (arXiv 2304.04687): two 1 MP IR cameras (OV9282), **5 cm baseline**, IR flood plus dot emitters for texture, no depth map, table plane from a chessboard homography. About 50k stereo pairs from 13 volunteers. In a 384-trial study, 96.35% had exactly one contact, 2.6% none, 1.04% multiple, and 98% of touches landed within 1 cm, with a mean error of about 4.5–4.9 mm. Their comparison table: Wilson (depth) 7 mm; Xiao DIRECT (IR + depth) 4.8 mm / 99.3%; MRTouch (HoloLens) 5 mm / 72% — [arXiv 2304.04687](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04687)
- Goussies et al. say earlier camera methods are often imprecise at separating hover from touch, and that Visual Touchpad and Wren et al. used very low-resolution images — [arXiv 2304.04687](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04687)

### Inferences
- The depth noise is σ_Z ≈ Z²·σ_d / (f·B). With Agarwal's numbers, B = 12 cm at Z = 50 cm gives 5 mm per disparity pixel. The user's 1 cm phone stereo (POCO logical camera 4) at about 1 m is 12× smaller baseline and 2× farther, so about 4× Z² / 12× B ≈ **~25 cm per disparity pixel** at similar focal length. That is hopeless for hover, as observed.
- For phone + Mac two-view triangulation, the ~4 cm depth noise likely comes from three things: (1) landmark noise of ~0.5% of width (~5–6 mm on a ~1 m wide image) per view, amplified by the ray intersection angle; (2) **no frame synchronisation**, where at 0.3–0.5 m/s finger speed a 33 ms offset is 10–17 mm; (3) MediaPipe fingertip landmarks are not the same physical point in both views (each is the silhouette tip from that viewpoint, and the finger is ~15 mm thick). Agarwal's fix was to triangulate many surface points with a finger model, not one landmark.

### Gaps
- Visual Touchpad's exact baseline and camera height were not stated in the readable text.

## 4. Deep-learning monocular touch / hover

### Takeaway
Learned monocular RGB touch works where the training set matches the viewpoint and surface. EgoTouch (skin, headset cameras) gets about 96–97% TP with about 3–6% FP. I found no published single-RGB model for touch vs hover on a projected wall seen from the projector side, and no public dataset for that setup. A custom model would need self-collected, sensor-labelled data.

### Cited Findings
- EgoTouch (Mollyn & Harrison, CMU, UIST 2024) uses the headset's own RGB cameras for on-skin touch. It reports **96.4% TP / 5.6% FP**, or 97.6% TP / 2.6% FP after correcting ground-truth sync error. Force estimation MAE is 6.8%, and light-vs-hard touch classification is 98%. It runs at ≥90 FPS and is calibration-free. Training covered 15 users with different skin tones and hair density, with labels from a finger/palm sensor rather than hand annotation. It does not work in the dark (night-vision cameras proposed) — [arXiv 2509.01786](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01786v1); [CMU news](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2024/november/cmus-egotouch-creates-simple-interfaces-for-virtual-and-augmented-reality); [FIG Lab](https://www.figlab.com/research/2024/egotouch)
- EgoTouch's comparison table gives 95.6% for its standing/walking, indoor/outdoor condition — [arXiv 2509.01786](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01786v1)
- UbiTouch (arXiv 2412.10565) uses a single *thermal* camera to tell hover from touch and recognise multi-finger input, and also evaluates simple RGB cameras as an alternative. Thermal residue on contact is the cue RGB lacks — [arXiv 2412.10565](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10565)
- Goussies et al. (stereo IR, ML) used about 50k frames, 13 people, 3 devices and 14 tasks, with labels from task design (instructed touch vs instructed hover) — [arXiv 2304.04687](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04687)

### Inferences
- EgoTouch works partly because skin deforms visibly under touch, and the camera is close (~30–50 cm) at a fixed egocentric viewpoint. A finger on a rigid wall shows almost no deformation, so a monocular model must rely on the shadow, the nail's blanching, or finger pose. Those cues are weak from a camera near the projector, which sees the finger head-on with its shadow hidden.
- A learned model is worth trying only after fixing the geometry (camera off-axis from the projector), since it would then learn the finger–shadow relation. Labels could come from a cheap contact sensor: a conductive-paint/foil pad, or a phone accelerometer on a thin board picking up taps. EgoTouch used this auto-labelling approach.

### Gaps
- I found no "single RGB camera touch detection" paper for projected walls from 2019–2025 with numbers, despite targeted searches. No public projector-wall touch/hover dataset was found.

## 5. Structured-light / projector-as-sensor tricks and commercial alternatives

### Takeaway
The projector can be the sensor. If it hides a binary code in its image, an off-axis camera can see the code shift on a finger that is above the wall, and that shift is zero at contact. Dai & Chung built this with just a projector and camera. It needs the camera off the projector's axis, the same geometry rule as shadows. Commercial products avoid RGB entirely (IR curtain, or IR plus camera), or use dwell.

### Cited Findings
- Dai & Chung, "Making any planar surface into a touch-sensitive display by a mere projector and camera" (CVPRW/PROCAMS 2012), and "Touchscreen Everywhere" (IEEE T-Cybernetics 2014, doi 10.1109/TCYB.2013.2284512). A random binary pattern is embedded at pixel accuracy as imperceptible structured light inside the normal displayed image. Touch is decided from the **disparity of pattern codes on the fingertip**: on the wall the disparity is zero, above it the disparity is nonzero. They evaluate display quality, hand segmentation, touch detection, tracking and multitouch — [project page](https://jwdai.github.io/Research/TouchEverywhere/TouchEverywhere.htm); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24196981/); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/cvprw/2012/dai2012cvprw-making); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/any-surface-a-touchscreen)
- Their slide deck reportedly gives error rates of about 0.8% and 3.3% for the tested surfaces, but the extracted text was garbled and the metric is unclear. **Unverified** — [slides](https://www.sambuz.com/doc/making-any-planar-surface-into-a-touch-sensitive-display-presentation-128975)
- VIRTOS briefly changes the projected button colour so shadow can be separated from finger, a simple form of temporal coding of projector output — [VIRTOS PDF](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/47286/47286.pdf)
- Commercial: Epson IR laser curtain (parallel light plane); Sony Xperia Touch IR + camera at 60 fps, close range only — [Epson manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/819888/Epson-Eb-595wi.html?page=81); [maketecheasier](https://maketecheasier.com/sony-xperia-touch-sony-projector)
- Pause/dwell detection as a click when contact can't be sensed — [Agarwal 2007](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Projected line trick, practical for this setup.** Project a thin bright horizontal line (or a stripe pattern) through a small window around the tracked fingertip, on dark surroundings. From a camera offset from the projector by angle Δ, the line on the finger appears shifted from where it lands on the wall by about h·tan Δ. At contact the shift is zero, since the line on the fingertip and on the wall coincide. That is one-image, sub-pixel-measurable structured light that needs no IR. It is the same physics as the shadow gap, but brighter and higher contrast because the projector controls the light. It still needs Δ of roughly 20–45°. The camera *near the projector* removes the parallax, so this trick also fails there.
- The pattern needs a few frames, so camera-projector frame sync limits latency. Webcam (30 fps) and projector (60 Hz) without genlock mean flashing patterns for 2–3 frames, or keeping a static local pattern under the finger.
- Alternatives that skip true contact: dwell (hold ~500–800 ms), a pinch or thumb gesture via MediaPipe as "click" with fingertip position as the cursor, or "lift-off" (a large, fast fingertip motion away from the wall). These are robust with today's setup and are how many camera-only systems ship.

### Gaps
- No imperceptible high-speed structured-light paper with a phone/webcam was verified. Dai & Chung's camera/projector offset and exact accuracy were not readable.

## 6. Geometric guidance: landmark noise ~0.5% of width, target <1 cm hover resolution

### Takeaway
To resolve 1 cm of height with ~5–6 mm landmark noise, the measured quantity must change by ≥ ~1.5–2 cm per cm of height. That takes either two views converging at ≥ ~45° each from the wall normal on opposite sides (and synchronised), or one camera ≥ ~30–45° off the light source's axis measuring the finger-to-shadow (or finger-to-projected-line) gap. A camera near the projector cannot do it at any resolution.

### Cited Findings
- Reference points: stereo 12 cm baseline at 50 cm gives 5 mm/disparity-pixel, needing a finger model to reach 2–3 mm [Agarwal 2007](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf). PlayAnywhere single camera plus off-axis IR gives ~3–4 mm at 640×480 [US7599561B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7599561B2/en). Visual Touchpad stereo threshold is ~1 cm [Malik & Laszlo](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~jflaszlo/papers/icmi-pui-2004/malik_2004_ICMI_visual_touchpad.pdf). A 5 cm stereo-IR + ML system gives 96% contact detection [Goussies](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04687).

### Inferences (my own derivation; not from a source)
- **Noise budget.** 0.5% of a ~1.2 m wide view is σ ≈ 6 mm per view, per landmark. The user measured 0.6% of width, which is consistent.
- **Two-view plane-parallax (phone + Mac).** Map each view's fingertip to wall coordinates with its ArUco homography. A point at height h then lands at foot + h·t₁ in view 1 and foot + h·t₂ in view 2, where tᵢ is the tan of each camera's viewing angle from the wall normal (a vector). Their difference is |Δp| = h·|t₁ − t₂|, with noise ≈ √2·σ ≈ 8.5 mm. For a 1 cm hover to be separable at ~2σ (≈ 95%), |t₁ − t₂| ≥ 2·8.5/10 ≈ 1.7. Cameras on **opposite sides** at ±40° give 0.84 + 0.84 = 1.68; ±45° gives 2.0. At 3σ (reliable) you need ±52°. If both cameras are on the same side, or one is near the projector at 0–10°, |t₁ − t₂| is small. For example 10° and 35° gives 0.18 + 0.70 = 0.52, so the 1σ height noise is about 8.5/0.52 ≈ **1.6 cm**, getting worse with sync and landmark-bias errors. That fits the ~4 cm the user observed.
- **Plus must-fix systematic errors:** hardware or timestamp sync between views (or only judge "touch" when the fingertip velocity is about 0, i.e. at rest on the wall), and landmark bias between views, which grows at oblique angles. Calibrate this by having the user press known targets and fitting a per-view offset as a function of hand pose.
- **Single camera + shadow (light from the projector).** Gap = h·|t_proj − t_cam|, measured within one image, so noise is σ_gap ≈ a few mm (shadow-tip localisation, not MediaPipe). With the projector near on-axis (t ≈ 0) and the camera at 35–45° (t ≈ 0.7–1.0), 1 cm of hover gives 7–10 mm of gap, enough for a 1 cm threshold if the shadow is segmented cleanly, e.g. with a white patch projected around the fingertip. The phone near the projector gives |t_proj − t_cam| ≈ 0.05–0.1, so 1 cm gives ≤ 1 mm, below noise. This explains the failure.
- **Grazing camera.** Height maps to image height by about cos β, where β is the elevation above the wall plane. At β ≤ 20°, 1 cm of hover gives ≥ 9 mm of image gap with only ~1 view needed, but coverage and occlusion are poor.
- **Practical recommendation (inference).** (1) Move the phone (or Mac) well off the projector axis: ≥ 35° from the wall normal at the touch area, e.g. about 0.8–1 m to the side at ~1.2 m from the wall. Project a white halo or a thin line around the tracked fingertip and threshold the fingertip-to-shadow/line gap. (2) Or use two views on opposite sides at ≥ ±45° with synchronised capture. (3) Always add temporal logic: touch = gap below threshold AND fingertip speed ≈ 0 for ≥ 2–3 frames. Release is hysteresis-based. (4) Keep a dwell/pinch fallback. Realistic expectation from the literature: button-level (≥ 5–8 cm targets) touch at ~90–95% with RGB shadows (VIRTOS-level). Mm-level touch like PlayAnywhere/Agarwal needs a dedicated off-axis illuminant (an IR LED plus the phone's IR-sensitive camera, or a visible LED) or a finger-model stereo approach.

### Gaps
- The numbers above are first-order geometry. They ignore lens distortion, finger thickness (~15 mm, which biases the silhouette tip toward the camera), and MediaPipe's depth-dependent bias. They should be checked against a quick measured experiment: press vs 1 cm vs 2 cm hover at fixed points, logging gap vs height.

## 7. Kit-only options (POCO M4 Pro + projector + Mac, no extra sensors): papers, repos, what to build, expected accuracy

### Takeaway
With only this kit, three approaches are physically sound. In order of promise:
- **(A) Audio-gated touch.** The camera gives *where*, the phone or Mac mic gives *when*, and a wall tap is a sharp transient. The idea has prior art (Intel patent, Sound MagicBoard, Toffee) and needs no new geometry.
- **(B) Projector-as-light with the phone moved ≥ 35° off the projector axis.** Measure the fingertip-to-shadow or fingertip-to-projected-line gap (PlayAnywhere / VIRTOS / Dai & Chung physics).
- **(C) Grazing phone view, optionally with the torch as a grazing light.** Measure the finger-to-shadow or finger-to-reflection meeting (ShinyTouch idea).

Pure monocular ML from the current near-projector position is unlikely to work; no paper shows it. Every hobby repo found either uses an LED pen, a laser, a depth sensor, or skips contact detection and uses gestures.

### Cited Findings: GitHub repos (checked 2026-11-04 via GitHub API)
- **diorwave/shinytouch** (Python/OpenCV, 28★, first written 2009, revived 2026). "Open Source touchscreen utility with no external hardware except a webcam", which "turns any flat reflective surface into a touch interface". It uses a webcam at an angle to a glossy surface and the finger's reflection. Code is old `opencv.cv` Python 2 bindings, so treat it as a reference, not drop-in — [github.com/diorwave/shinytouch](https://github.com/diorwave/shinytouch)
- **huzz/Touch-Projectors** (Python, 40★, 2018) and its near-twin **kushalchaudhari21/iPlanes-InteractivePlanes** (2020). Webcam plus 4-point ROI homography plus pyautogui. They need an **LED pen with a push-button**, so the "touch" is the LED lighting up, not vision contact detection — [huzz/Touch-Projectors](https://github.com/huzz/Touch-Projectors); [iPlanes](https://github.com/kushalchaudhari21/iPlanes-InteractivePlanes)
- **Shiba-Lab/LaserTouch** (C++, 2019). "Turn any surface to a touchscreen with projector, camera and laser module". It uses a line-laser light plane, so it is outside "no extra sensors" but is the reference design for a light plane — [Shiba-Lab/LaserTouch](https://github.com/Shiba-Lab/LaserTouch)
- **dschagen/SurfaceOS** (Python, ShellHacks 2026). Webcam with MediaPipe gesture recognizer, projector, and corner/marker calibration. It uses **gestures plus hold-to-select (dwell)**, not contact detection. It is the closest modern MediaPipe + projector pipeline to copy for calibration/UI plumbing — [dschagen/SurfaceOS](https://github.com/dschagen/SurfaceOS)
- **SAB123-D/-Smart-Projector-Touch-Control** (2025) describes turning a projector screen "into a smart, touch-like interactive surface using a mobile phone camera". The README was not inspected and the method is unverified — [repo](https://github.com/SAB123-D/-Smart-Projector-Touch-Control)
- **kdv123/SurfaceTap** (Python). Acoustic tap localisation with TDOA solvers (Newton, least-squares, fsolve) and real-time TCP clients, using **contact microphones on the surface**. Its TDOA/onset code is reusable for phone+Mac mics — [kdv123/SurfaceTap](https://github.com/kdv123/SurfaceTap)
- **sodipak420-creator/phantom-touch-android** describes an "Acoustic surface-tap gesture controller for Android using hardware linear acceleration". It is unverified, but shows phone-IMU tap sensing is being tried — [repo](https://github.com/sodipak420-creator/phantom-touch-android)
- **torinmb/mediapipe-touchdesigner** is a MediaPipe-in-TouchDesigner plugin, relevant because the user works in TD. It does no contact detection itself — [ITP session page mentioning it](https://itp.nyu.edu/camp/2026/session/86)
- No public code was found for Goussies et al. (Google, stereo-IR touch), EgoTouch, PlayAnywhere or Dai & Chung — [arXiv 2304.04687](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04687)

### Cited Findings: acoustic "when" + camera "where"
- Intel patent US9971457 "Audio augmentation of touch detection for surfaces". A camera tracks the fingertip near the surface, the microphone is armed when the finger approaches, and a touch is declared only when vision sees motion toward the surface AND the mic picks up a vibration. It is framed as improving "time of touch" accuracy — [US9971457B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9971457)
- US8411050: a microphone detects the sound of a finger on a projected touch region as a key press, with loudness indicating tap strength — [US20110084914A1](https://www.google.de/patents/US20110084914)
- US11061514: acoustic touch detection combined with a projector-camera subsystem, where the camera fingertip refines the acoustic position — [US11061514](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11061514)
- "Sound MagicBoard" (ICMI 2000) uses multi-microphone phase to localise taps and supports a vision finger tracker on a whiteboard — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/3-540-40063-X_9.pdf)
- Toffee (Xiao, Lew, Marsanico, Hariharan, Hudson, Harrison, MobileHCI 2014) does acoustic TDOA from a device resting on a hard table, with a **mean bearing error of 4.3°** in a laptop prototype. It needs only "a hard tabletop and gravity" for coupling — [Toffee page](https://www.robertxiao.ca/research/toffee/); [PDF](https://robertxiao.ca/pubs/2014_MobileHCI_Toffee.pdf); [FIG Lab](https://www.figlab.com/research/2014/toffee)
- ALTo (2021): 4 piezo contact mics, TDOA, error 1.45 cm (x) / 2.72 cm (y) — [arXiv 2108.06837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.06837)
- Acustico (Meta, UIST 2020): wrist-worn passive acoustic TDOA, tap detection **F1 = 0.9987**, localisation RMS 7.6 mm (x) / 4.6 mm (y), 20 participants — [Meta research](https://research.facebook.com/publications/acustico-surface-tap-detection-and-localization-using-wrist-based-acoustic-tdoa-sensing/)
- Simple accelerometer/mic thresholding trades false positives against misses, and noise causes false taps — [US8326569 Tap detection](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8326569)

### Inferences: concrete builds for this kit (expected accuracies are my estimates, not measured)
1. **Audio-gated touch: build first, highest confidence.**
   - *Build:* MediaPipe fingertip (existing) as cursor. Mac mic and/or the phone's mic streamed over the existing WebSocket. Run onset detection (high-pass > 1–2 kHz, spectral flux or energy ratio over ~5 ms) and accept a tap only if (a) the fingertip is inside the wall ROI, (b) fingertip speed toward the wall dropped to about 0 within ±50 ms of the onset, and (c) the gap/hover score from any other cue is "near". Better still, rest the **phone on or against the wall or its frame** so the IMU/mic picks up structure-borne vibration (Toffee-style coupling).
   - *Expected:* tap *events* (not drags) detected at roughly 90–98%, judging by Acustico/Intel-style fusion. False positives come from speech, claps and keyboard; the vision gate removes most of them. Drags/long presses still need a hover cue or "tap to start, lift to end" logic.
   - *Limits:* soft taps on drywall are quiet. Users must tap, not lightly touch. Mac audio latency is ~10–30 ms; align by timestamps, not frames.
2. **Projector-as-light-source shadow or line gap, with the phone moved off-axis.**
   - *Build:* projector straight-on. Put the phone on a tripod 0.8–1.2 m to the side (≥ 35° off the wall normal at the UI), not beside the projector. Each frame, project a **small white halo** (or a thin bright horizontal line) centred on the last fingertip estimate, with the rest of the UI as normal. In the phone image, segment the dark shadow (or the bright line's break on the finger) next to the fingertip and measure the gap in wall coordinates through the ArUco homography. Touch = gap < threshold for ≥ 2 frames with fingertip speed about 0, plus hysteresis.
   - *Expected:* with tan Δ ≈ 0.7–1, a 1 cm hover gives 7–10 mm of gap. Button-level (≥ 5 cm targets) touch at roughly 90–95% (VIRTOS-like), and possibly finger-level ~1 cm hover resolution if shadow segmentation is clean. PlayAnywhere's 3–4 mm needed a dedicated IR LED, so don't expect that.
   - *Refs:* [US7599561B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7599561B2/en), [VIRTOS](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/47286/47286.pdf), [Dai & Chung](https://jwdai.github.io/Research/TouchEverywhere/TouchEverywhere.htm).
3. **Grazing phone view: shadow, torch or reflection meeting.**
   - *Build:* phone at the wall edge, lens 2–5 cm out from the wall, looking along it (elevation ≤ 15–20°). Optionally turn on the **phone torch** as a grazing light from beside the lens, so a hovering finger casts a shadow on the wall *below or beside it* in the image. A touch is when finger silhouette and shadow (or, on a glossy surface, the finger's mirror reflection, as in ShinyTouch) meet. Measure the pixel gap between fingertip and the wall line or its reflection.
   - *Expected:* near-direct height measurement (1 cm hover ≈ 1 cm image gap close to the phone). Good for a strip UI within ~1 m of the phone, poor coverage at the far end, and occlusion when several fingers are present. The torch helps only near the phone because illumination falls off with distance².
   - *Refs:* [shinytouch](https://github.com/diorwave/shinytouch), [Epson curtain parallel-plane requirement](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/819888/Epson-Eb-595wi.html?page=81).
4. **High-fps phone capture.** Higher fps helps the *timing* cues: the fingertip decelerating to zero at contact, and the small rebound or "squash" of the fingertip pad. It also reduces cross-view sync error, since a 240 fps offset of ≤ 4 ms is ≤ 2 mm at 0.5 m/s. It does not add height parallax. Android exposes this through Camera2 constrained high-speed sessions, which are usually 720p and recording-oriented. Live streaming at 120/240 fps from this phone is unverified (the POCO logical camera 4 stereo stream was only ~18 fps in earlier tests).
5. **Two-view (phone + Mac), redone properly.** Only worthwhile if both cameras sit on opposite sides at ≥ ±45° from the wall normal and are time-synced (flash a projected sync pattern, or timestamp-align with NTP-like offsets). Use a finger-surface model (Agarwal) rather than a single MediaPipe landmark. The MacBook webcam is fixed to the laptop lid, so this geometry is awkward and option 2 is easier.
6. **ML touch classifier.** Train only *after* the geometry gives a real cue (option 2 or 3). Collect auto-labelled data using the audio-tap detector from option 1 as the label source for "contact" frames: tap = contact, frames ≥ 300 ms before or after = hover. This mirrors EgoTouch's sensor-labelled training ([arXiv 2509.01786](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01786v1)). Input is a small crop around the fingertip (finger plus shadow), with a tiny CNN or gradient-boosted features (gap, shadow area, fingertip speed). Expected performance is unknown. No published projector-wall RGB baseline exists.
7. **Fallbacks that ship today:** dwell (hold 500–800 ms, as SurfaceOS does), a pinch or thumb-tap gesture as the click, or a "push" gesture (fingertip landmark z / hand scale change). These are robust with the current setup.

### Gaps
- No acoustic study found measures finger taps on a *wall* sensed by a *phone or laptop mic in the air* (not contact-coupled). The detection rate for soft taps on drywall is unknown and should be measured directly.
- POCO M4 Pro high-speed capture modes over Camera2 (resolution/fps available to apps, and whether they stream live) were not verified.
- The method of SAB123-D's phone-camera projector-touch repo was not inspected.
