# Move the light, not the math: wall touch

With only the POCO M4 Pro, the projector and the Mac, no published system turns an ordinary wall into a fast (under 50 ms), accurate, bare-finger tap-and-draw surface. The research shows exactly why: telling touch from hover is a geometry problem, not an algorithm problem. Every working camera method measures how far something moves sideways when the finger lifts 1 cm. That movement comes from the angle between the light and the camera, or between two cameras, and in every setup tested today that angle was close to zero or swamped by noise. The camera already answers *where* well enough: 0.6% of wall width is about 6 mm on a 1 m wall, which is near the published 4.5–7 mm range for good systems. What is missing is a reliable *when*. With the current kit there are two physically sound ways to get it. The first is an **audio or vibration onset from the phone taped to the wall**, which can fire a tap in well under 50 ms because it does not wait for camera frames. The second is a **projector-drawn halo or line around the fingertip, seen by the phone placed at least 35° off the projector axis**, which keeps a touch "down" for drawing. The cheapest real fix costs **₹32–64: one or two 650 nm red line lasers** grazing the wall. That turns touch into bright-blob detection, the way Epson and BenQ do it, with hold and drag included. The recommended build adds the laser plane to SurfaceTouch first, with audio onset as the latency path and pinch-to-click kept as the fallback.

## Today's failures follow one equation about parallax

A fingertip hovering at height *h* lands on the wall image at a different point from where it would touch. How far depends on the viewing angles. Map every view onto the wall with the ArUco homography. A camera at angle θ from the wall normal then sees the fingertip displaced by about *h*·tan θ. A light source at angle φ throws the finger's shadow *h*·tan φ away. So the only signals that grow with hover height are differences between angles: the finger-to-shadow gap is *h*·|tan φ − tan θ|, and the disagreement between two views is *h*·|tan θ₁ − tan θ₂|. Every working shadow system follows this rule. PlayAnywhere put its IR illuminant "off axis from the single camera" and decided touch when finger and shadow "come together", reaching about **3–4 mm precision at 640×480** ([US7599561B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7599561B2/en)). VIRTOS and Everywhere Switch offset an RGB camera **about 50 cm from a projector 400 cm away**. That is only about 7°, so they fell back to judging whole 8–12 cm buttons by shadow area, with **6–9% button failures** ([VIRTOS](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2014/47286/47286.pdf); [Everywhere Switch](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2015/53076/53076.pdf)).

Plugging today's setups into that equation explains each result. **Shadow gap with the phone near the projector:** |tan φ − tan θ| is about 0.05–0.1, so a 1 cm hover makes a gap of 1 mm or less. That is below the noise of finding the shadow tip, so the shadow hides behind the finger at every height and the result was near chance. **1 cm phone stereo:** depth noise scales as Z²/(f·B). Agarwal et al. needed a 12 cm baseline at 50 cm just to get **5 mm per disparity pixel**, and called plain disparity "centimetre level precision at best" ([Agarwal 2007](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf)). A 1 cm baseline at about 1 m is 12× smaller and twice as far away. That works out to roughly 25 cm per disparity pixel, which matches the ~1.4 px per 3 cm logged in SurfaceTouch's own notes. **Phone + Mac two-view (~4 cm depth noise):** both cameras sat on the same side of the wall normal at small angles. For example, 10° and 35° gives |tan θ₁ − tan θ₂| ≈ 0.52. With about 8.5 mm of combined landmark noise, that alone means about **1.6 cm of height noise (1σ)**. Three more errors pile on top. The two cameras were not frame-synced, and one 45 ms frame at 22 fps is 13–23 mm of finger travel at 0.3–0.5 m/s. The two MediaPipe tips are also different physical points on a 15 mm-thick finger, because each is the silhouette tip from its own viewpoint. Agarwal reached 2–3 mm only by fitting many stereo points to a finger model, not one landmark ([Agarwal 2007](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mkchandraker/pdf/tabletop07_touchsensing.pdf)).

**The side-angle view (separation 0.53)** is the instructive failure, because its geometry was nearly right: two views about 90° apart, so a hover should appear almost full-size. In practice the phone could not read the markers at a grazing angle, so its wall model came from six finger touches. Any error in the Mac's 6 mm fingertip estimate, the unsynced timing, or MediaPipe's bias at oblique views then went straight into the "expected touch point" the phone compared against. The cue was being measured against a reference noisier than the cue itself. Commercial products that do work along the wall avoid this by measuring *light* in a plane parallel to the surface, not a camera's estimate of a landmark. Epson warns that if its laser "is not emitted parallel to the screen" touch is detected incorrectly ([Epson manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/819888/Epson-Eb-595wi.html?page=81)). **Pinch-as-click works for the opposite reason.** Thumb-to-index distance is a 2D measurement inside one hand, inside one image, at full resolution, and it needs no depth or wall reference at all.

## What published systems prove is reachable, and at what cost

Depth-camera systems set the benchmark, and the numbers show how fine the margin is. Wilson's Kinect touch band was **3–14 mm above the table at 0.75 m and 6–30 mm at 1.5 m**. In his words, "it is impossible to say whether an observed object is in contact from the depth image alone" ([Wilson ITS 2010](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/WilsonITS2010.pdf)). OmniTouch called a finger "clicked" at 1 cm or less and "hovering" above 2 cm, and got **96.5% exactly-one-click** across 6,048 trials. On the wall, touches landed 10 mm low, and a 16.2 mm button was needed for 95% hits ([OmniTouch](https://www.chrisharrison.net/projects/omnitouch/omnitouch.pdf)). DIRECT fused Kinect v2 depth with IR edges for **4.8 mm error, 99.3% detection and 15 ms latency**. Plain background subtraction on the same data had over 40 mm error, and moving the threshold by 1 mm caused "hundreds of errant touches … every second" ([DIRECT](https://robertxiao.ca/pubs/2016_ISS_DIRECT.pdf)). Learned systems do as well only where the geometry already gives a cue. A 5 cm-baseline stereo-IR rig with dot projectors reached 96% single-contact detection with 98% of touches within 1 cm ([Goussies et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04687)). EgoTouch reached 96.4% true positives with a headset RGB camera, on skin that visibly deforms ([EgoTouch](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.01786v1)). EclipseTouch's IR "structured shadows" reached **98.0% touch accuracy, with an M2 running inference in 0.47 ms** ([EclipseTouch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03430)). No paper or public dataset shows monocular RGB touch on a projected wall seen from the projector side. The closest monocular result, a fine-tuned Depth Anything V2 contact detector, reaches 94% F1 only at **25–45 cm** using its own training geometry ([CVPR 2026 project](https://muxiddin19.github.io/Multimodal-Fingertip-Contact-Detection-via-Depth-and-Motion-Fusion)).

Three theoretical routes stay open with the current kit. Dai & Chung hid a random binary pattern in the projected image and decided touch from whether the **code disparity on the fingertip is zero (on the wall) or not (above it)**, using a projector and camera only ([Touchscreen Everywhere](https://jwdai.github.io/Research/TouchEverywhere/TouchEverywhere.htm)). Gray-code projector-to-phone structured light with a 30–50 cm baseline could, on paper, resolve 0.5–2 mm of depth at 1.5 m. That is my own triangulation arithmetic, limited in practice by sync and defocus, with open procam tools such as [ProCamToolkit](https://github.com/YCAMInterlab/ProCamToolkit). Both need the camera off the projector axis, and both cost projector frames that a 22 fps phone stream can barely spare. Acoustic sensing is the third route, and the fastest. Toffee showed a device coupled to a hard surface by gravity alone can sense taps on it ([Toffee](https://www.robertxiao.ca/research/toffee/)). Acustico reached **tap detection F1 = 0.9987** ([Meta research](https://research.facebook.com/publications/acustico-surface-tap-detection-and-localization-using-wrist-based-acoustic-tdoa-sensing/)). Intel patented the exact fusion this project needs: the camera tracks the fingertip, and touch is declared only when the fingertip moves toward the surface *and* a vibration arrives ([US9971457](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9971457)). The catch is that acoustics see only the impact. Elo's own patent admits bending-wave systems cannot detect a stationary hold or a clean release ([US8659579B2](https://www.google.co.uk/patents/US8659579)). And no paper measures soft fingertip taps on a painted masonry wall.

Hobby code confirms the gap rather than closing it. [huzz/Touch-Projectors](https://github.com/huzz/Touch-Projectors) uses a push-button LED pen. [dschagen/SurfaceOS](https://github.com/dschagen/SurfaceOS) uses MediaPipe plus dwell. [diorwave/shinytouch](https://github.com/diorwave/shinytouch) uses a fingertip's reflection on a glossy surface, which is old Python 2 code but a valid idea. [Shiba-Lab/LaserTouch](https://github.com/Shiba-Lab/LaserTouch) is the laser-plane reference design. Reusable pieces for the audio route are [kdv123/SurfaceTap](https://github.com/kdv123/SurfaceTap) (onset and TDOA code), [nneonneo/direct-handtracking](https://github.com/nneonneo/direct-handtracking) (the DIRECT pipeline) and Google's [Oboe](https://developer.android.com/games/sdk/oboe) for low-latency Android audio.

## Ranked builds: four kit-only methods, one ₹32 upgrade

The ranking weighs physical soundness, the 50 ms budget and how much SurfaceTouch already has. One fact constrains everything. **The phone streams at about 22 fps, so each frame is about 45 ms, and the state machine's 100 ms confirm-down alone is already double the budget.** Any camera-only "when" signal lands at 70–150 ms. Getting under 50 ms needs one of two things: a non-camera onset that fires on arrival using the last known fingertip position, or a cue clean enough to confirm in one frame at 60 fps or more. The accuracy figures below are estimates from the cited geometry, not measurements.

| Rank | Method (kit-only unless noted) | Tap | Draw / hold | Likely latency | Expected accuracy | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Red 650 nm line laser plane (₹32–64 add-on)**, watched by the phone at low manual exposure | Yes | Yes (blob persists) | 1 frame + transfer; under 50 ms at 60 fps with blob detection on the phone | About 5–10 mm after homography; hover false-triggers at 3–8 mm sheet height | Wall flatness; projected red content; no published phone + red-plane precedent |
| 2 | **Audio/vibration onset from the phone taped to the wall** + MediaPipe position | Yes | Start only; hold must come from another cue | Roughly 15–45 ms (5–20 ms buffer + Wi-Fi + onset) | Position = today's 0.6% width; detection unknown on painted wall | Soft fingertip taps may be inaudible; needs a firm tap |
| 3 | **Active halo/line shadow** with the phone ≥35° off the projector axis (current `shadow.py`, fixed geometry) | Yes | Yes | 70–150 ms at 22 fps with current confirm timers | Button-level (≥5 cm) at roughly 90–95%; maybe 1 cm hover resolution | Shadow segmentation on busy content |
| 4 | **Grazing phone** (lens 2–5 cm from the wall), torch as grazing light or glossy-wall reflection | Yes | Yes | As rank 3 | 1 cm hover ≈ 1 cm image gap near the phone | Narrow strip of coverage, occlusion |
| 5 | Two-view, redone: opposite sides at ±45°, synced, finger model | Yes | Yes | As rank 3 | Needs \|tan θ₁ − tan θ₂\| ≥ 1.7 for 1 cm at 2σ | MacBook lid can't be placed at 45° easily |
| — | Fallback: pinch-as-click (already working), dwell | Yes | Yes (pinch-drag) | Current | Proven today | Not "touch" |

The laser ranks first because it changes what is being measured. A sheet about 1 mm thick, a few mm off the wall, lights the fingertip only when it breaks the plane. The NUI Group handbook calls the result "very high contrast blobs with very little effort" ([NUI Group handbook](https://multigesture.net//wp-content/uploads/2009/07/First_Edition_Community_Release.pdf)). The pipeline is about 100–200 lines of background subtraction, thresholding, connected components and homography, and SurfaceTouch already has the homography. The phone's IR-cut filter is irrelevant at 650 nm. To separate laser scatter from the projected image, lock exposure low, threshold red-minus-green, subtract an empty-hand frame, and keep saturated red out of touch zones. Commercial units tilt the sheet from **about 8 mm at the top to 2 mm at the bottom** and want the wall flat within about 3 mm ([Epson FTU addendum](https://www.epson.co.nz/d/pub/epson/techtips/installation%20manual%20addendum%20-%20rev_c.pdf)). An ordinary painted wall will need shims and probably a 3–8 mm sheet, which means some hover false-triggers. A single laser hides a second finger behind the first, so two corner modules are the norm for multi-touch ([NUI Group handbook](https://multigesture.net//wp-content/uploads/2009/07/First_Edition_Community_Release.pdf)). I found no source showing a visible-red *plane* used against a live projection with a phone camera, so the ₹32 bench test is the first experiment, not an assumption.

Audio ranks second and is the only kit-only path that clearly beats 50 ms. Do the onset detection **on the phone**. Use `AudioRecord` with the UNPROCESSED source, so voice processing doesn't remove taps, and send only `{onset_time, energy}` over UDP. Android's `AudioRecord.getTimestamp` stamps frames "at the earliest point available in the capture pipeline" ([Android reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/AudioRecord#getTimestamp(android.media.AudioTimestamp,%20int))), so Wi-Fi jitter delays delivery but does not corrupt timing. The accelerometer is capped at **200 Hz on Android 12+** without `HIGH_SAMPLING_RATE_SENSORS` ([Expo docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v54.0.0/sdk/sensors)), which is too slow for anything but a thump. ViBand showed that sample rate, not sensitivity, is what limits IMU tap sensing ([ViBand](https://www.robertxiao.ca/research/viband/)). Coupling decides everything, so the phone should be taped flat to the wall within about 0.5–1 m of the touch area. The MacBook's airborne mics are useful only as a noise reference. Drawing is the weak spot. Acoustics give the stroke start, and ScratchInput shows nail friction on wall paint does carry ([ScratchInput](https://www.chrisharrison.net/index.php/Research/ScratchInput)), but a still finger is silent. So audio starts the stroke and a camera cue or pinch must keep it alive.

The value curve drops steeply after the first rupee. The red laser and a piezo cost under ₹100 together and cover the two missing signals: a contact plane for draw, and an impact onset for fast taps. The MPR121 plus paint is the cheapest *true contact* sensor and solves hold/drag, which acoustics can't. The ₹10,000 IR frame is the cheapest option that works out of the box, but it stops being your own system. Depth cameras are poor value at 1.5 m. DIRECT needed Kinect v2 depth *and* IR edges to beat the 40 mm error of plain thresholding. Be careful with the IR route: off-the-shelf 850 nm modules are often Class 3R even at 5 mW ([Picotronic](https://shop.picotronic.de/en/p/70155488)), and invisible IR triggers no blink reflex ([NUI Group handbook](https://multigesture.net//wp-content/uploads/2009/07/First_Edition_Community_Release.pdf)). The visible red module is the safer one for prototyping, but never look into it.

## Build plan: laser plane first, audio for speed, shadow as the kit-only track

The recommendation is a fused "where + when" engine. MediaPipe and the ArUco homography stay as the source of *where*. The *when* comes from the laser blob (tap, hold, drag), with the phone-wall audio onset as the sub-50 ms trigger and pinch as the always-available fallback. If buying nothing at all is a hard rule, steps 4–6 still form a complete kit-only track. Each step reuses the recorder and replay tool, so every cue is judged on the same recorded sessions before it goes live.

| Step | Change to SurfaceTouch | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Measure the cue before building | Extend Experiment A to log raw cue value vs hover height (touch, 10, 20 mm) at 6 points for each candidate cue | Cue separates touch from 10 mm hover at 2σ or better on replay |
| 1. ₹32 laser bench test | Mount one 650 nm module at the top edge, 3–8 mm off the wall, tilted in (Epson 8→2 mm). Phone at fixed low ISO/exposure via Camera2. New `surfacetouch/laser.py`: empty-hand background, red−green threshold, `connectedComponentsWithStats`, blob → wall via existing homography | Blob visible on white and dark projected content; hover at 10 mm gives no blob |
| 2. Laser as the contact model | Feed blob presence (and blob-to-MediaPipe-tip distance under about 2 cm) into `contact.py` as p = 1/0. Cut `confirm_down` to 1 frame for laser blobs and keep the 120 ms confirm-up hysteresis in `state.py` | Experiment B: ≥95% detection, ≤2% false touches, median error ≤10 mm; Experiment C drawing with no broken strokes |
| 3. Hit the latency budget | Move blob detection onto the phone (`android/`) and send blob coordinates, not frames. Request 60 fps at reduced resolution. Timestamp frames on the phone | Recorder timestamps show motion-to-event under 50 ms (excluding projector lag) |
| 4. Audio onset trigger | Small Kotlin module: `AudioRecord` UNPROCESSED 48 kHz, high-pass above 1–2 kHz, energy/flux onset, UDP `{t_boottime_ns, energy}`. NTP-style ping for the clock offset. Mac side `surfacetouch/tap.py` accepts an onset only if the fingertip is in the ROI and decelerating within ±50 ms | Taped phone: ≥90% firm taps detected, under 1 false tap/min with speech and the projector fan |
| 5. Kit-only shadow fix | Move the phone ≥35° off the projector axis (0.8–1.2 m to the side at about 1.2 m). In `app.py`, render a small white halo or thin horizontal line around the last fingertip. Retrain `contact.py` on the gap (`sep_n`, `along_n`) | Step 0 criterion passes; button-level (≥5 cm) touch at 90% or more |
| 6. Auto-labelled ML | Use audio onsets (or laser blobs) as labels: onset = contact, frames ≥300 ms away = hover, as EgoTouch did with sensor labels. Train a small crop classifier on finger + shadow and replay old sessions | Beats the logistic model on held-out replays |
| 7. Keep the fallback | Pinch stays bound. Mode switch laser → shadow+audio → pinch, chosen at calibration by which cue passed step 0 | Never stuck, never unusable |

## Conclusion

The experiments did not fail because of MediaPipe, noise filtering or model choice. They failed because each setup measured a quantity that barely changes with hover height. The fix is to change what light does at the wall, either a plane parallel to it or a source well off the camera's axis, not to add more computation on top of the same pixels. That also explains why the cheapest serious upgrade is a ₹32 line laser rather than a ₹35,000 depth camera: at 1.5 m the depth camera would still need a second cue, while the laser *is* the cue. The 50 ms target is a separate constraint, set by the 22 fps stream and confirmation timers, and only an event-style signal (audio onset, or a one-frame laser blob detected on the phone) meets it.

Three things are unknown and only a bench session can settle them. Can the POCO see red laser scatter against a live projection? Can a wall-taped phone hear a fingertip pad on painted plaster? How flat is this particular wall? No published source answers any of them, so the build plan begins with step 0, measuring each cue against hover height, rather than with more features.
